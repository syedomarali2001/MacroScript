MACROSCRIPT - VERSION 6 OVERVIEW
================================
Completion date: 2026-11-06
Version status: Forecasting research and final methodological audit complete.
                NOT approved for reliable live recession-probability forecasts.

ONE-SENTENCE SUMMARY
--------------------
V6 tested whether the historical warning indicators developed in V5 could
produce reliable US recession-onset probabilities at 3-, 6-, and 12-month
horizons. Multiple models, time-ordered tests, alternative economic features,
ALFRED sensitivity checks, expanding-window refits, calibration and vintage-
origin training revealed some historical predictive signal but insufficient
robustness for deployment; the modelling work is archived and V7 becomes a
transparent economic-monitoring and historical-research application.


1. WHY WE BUILT V6
------------------
V1-V4 established economic-data retrieval, ALFRED historical vintages, quality
checking and descriptive economic features. V5 added recession-onset targets,
exploratory Treasury/housing/claims/credit warning-rule research, and an
approximately 1999-2024 point-in-time feature dataset.

V5 did NOT produce independently validated recession probabilities. Its
historical 8/8 rule detections used periods also examined when developing the
rules. V6 therefore asked:
  - Can probabilities beat a simple historical recession-event-rate baseline?
  - Can statistical or ML models improve on the V5 Treasury/housing rule?
  - Are 3/6/12-month probabilities well ranked AND well calibrated?
  - Does performance survive chronological training and ALFRED/PIT inputs?
  - Do interest-rate, credit, lagged or duration indicators help?
  - Are gains stable across historical decades and recent expansions?
  - Does training on origin-vintage observations change the conclusion?

A GOOD OUTCOME WAS AN HONEST MODEL VALIDATION DECISION, INCLUDING REJECTION
OF MODELS THAT DID NOT DEMONSTRATE SUFFICIENT RELIABILITY. V6 does not claim
any investment return, trading signal or dependable real-time recession call.


2. OUTCOME, INPUT DATA AND CHRONOLOGY
-------------------------------------
TARGET:
  NBER-dated US recession onset, represented by retrospective FRED USREC
  monthly labels. Predict an onset within months M+1 through M+3, M+6 or M+12.
  Recession months are excluded from onset-forecast evaluation. USREC is an
  outcome/evaluation label, NEVER an economic predictor. Future outcome labels
  must not be treated as known before they mature or are announced.

ORIGINAL FIVE FEATURES / SIX UNDERLYING MONTHLY SERIES:
  unemployment_sahm_pp          calculated from UNRATE
  payroll_growth_12m_pct        PAYEMS
  industrial_growth_12m_pct     INDPRO
  permits_growth_12m_pct        PERMIT
  treasury_spread_pp            GS10 - TB3MS

  Treasury spread values are percentage POINTS. Employment, production and
  building-permit growth are percentage changes. The monthly GS10-TB3MS
  proxy is not identical to the daily FRED T10Y3M series.

MAIN DATASET SOURCES:
  v5_research_revised.csv       756 monthly records, 1962-01 to 2024-12;
                                today's REVISED historical features.
  v5_pit_full_rows.csv          304 historical month-end ALFRED feature
                                snapshots, 1999-09 to 2024-12.
  v6_model_revised.csv          671 eligible revised expansion-month rows
                                after V6 exclusions and target requirements.
  v6_model_pit.csv              276 eligible historical PIT expansion-month
                                rows after quality and target requirements.

The forecasting experiment initially used fixed chronological boundaries:
  Training:    through 1988-12 (modelled training sample starts in 1962).
  Validation:  1990-01 through 2003-12.
  Test:        2005-01 through 2024-12.
  Purge gaps:  1989 and 2004 protect against overlapping forecast horizons.
  Actual model-specific sample counts can vary with data coverage.

IMPORTANT: 'Chronologically separated' is NOT synonymous with 'untouched'.
The 2005-2024 period had already been examined in V5 and during V6. Later
candidate exploration on that period introduces selection bias. The ALFRED
feature-input comparison is stronger than revised-only evaluation but still
is NOT a complete contemporaneous training/release-calendar replay.


3. THE EXPERIMENTS AND WHAT THEY TAUGHT US
-----------------------------------------
STEP 1 - Prepare audited modelling tables (model_data.py)
  - Created matched revised and PIT datasets with explicit source, month,
    as-of date and train/validation/test split metadata.
  - Kept only eligible expansion-month observations with complete targets.
  - Enforced nested 3/6/12-month labels and quality requirements for PIT.
  - Protected the intended train/validation/test boundaries with purge gaps.

STEP 2 - Fixed logistic regression and validation (forecast.py)
  - Fit three standardized, regularized logistic classifiers on training
    observations only, with horizon probabilities projected to nondecreasing
    3/6/12-month values where required.
  - Estimated baseline event probabilities and V5-rule conditional rates
    using training data rather than using future test prevalence.
  - The five-feature logistic model looked strong in validation, but this
    did NOT guarantee stable later performance.

STEP 3 - Frozen test and ALFRED feature-input audit (evaluate.py)
  - Evaluated the existing fitted models on matched 2005-2024 revised and
    PIT observations, reporting Brier score, log loss, AUC and warnings.
  - The encouraging validation result deteriorated substantially. This was
    a major reason NOT to deploy the original logistic model.
  - Model training had used revised historical predictors; replacing the
    test inputs with PIT inputs did not turn this into a fully PIT-trained
    historical backtest.

STEP 4/5 - Expanding-window logistic stress tests
  Files: walkforward.py, walkforward_test.py
  - Refit models chronologically using only labels judged mature under an
    initial 18-month proxy (12-month target plus a six-month buffer).
  - Monthly/expanding training did not remove instability or recent false
    alerts. That delay was an APPROXIMATION, not verified NBER announcement
    availability for each event.

STEP 6 - Alternative statistical models (alternatives.py)
  - Compared a Treasury-only probit, Treasury/housing regularized logistic,
    an early-trained two-feature control and chronological calibration.
  - Some alternatives were less poor than the original five-feature logistic
    in the later historical sample, but did not reliably beat the base-rate
    benchmark on the targeted 12-month PIT comparison.
  - Calibration could worsen losses even if ranking/AUC stayed respectable.

BROAD MODEL COMPARISON (model_experiments.py)
  - Compared logistic L2, elastic-net logistic, Random Forest, gradient
    boosting and simplified pooled hazard-style models across two-feature,
    five-feature and lagged/duration-augmented feature groups.
  - The five-feature Random Forest was much more stable than logistic, but
    its PIT 12-month result was only roughly comparable to the simple base.
  - Additional model complexity did not systematically outperform simple
    baselines. Pooled overlapping monthly outcomes must not be interpreted
    as independent recession events or formal coefficient inference.

ROUND A - Chronological expanding / two-year refit stress test
  File: research_rounds.py; outputs v6_roundA_*.csv/json
  - Tested several selected model families under repeated historical refits.
  - In this experiment, the five-feature models did not provide sufficient
    stable 12-month PIT skill relative to a historical event-rate baseline.

ROUND B - Additional economic indicators
  File: research_rounds.py; outputs v6_roundB_*.csv/json
  - Added GS1, FEDFUNDS, BAA and GS10, with predeclared lags, forming:
      short_curve_pp    = GS1 - FEDFUNDS
      funds_level_pp    = FEDFUNDS
      baa_treasury_pp   = BAA - GS10
  - Compared five-feature, seven-feature (add two rates), eight-feature
    (rates plus credit) and selected logistic/credit-only additions.
  - Interest-rate additions materially improved historical PIT test scores.
    HOWEVER the favourable later-period candidates were examined after the
    evaluation period was already known; selecting the best on that basis
    is not independent model validation.
  - The BAA-based PIT audit excludes three missing historical months in
    2016-12, 2017-01 and 2017-02: the shared comparison has 217 months.

ROUND C - Walk-forward Random Forest robustness
  File: robustness_round_c.py; outputs v6_roundC_*.csv/json
  - Compared the original five, seven rates and eight rates/credit features
    under 18-month-maturity expanding training and two-year model refits.
  - Checked early historical, validation and revised/PIT test intervals.
  - Seven/eight-feature models had promising later-period ranking and
    probability scores; both showed much more alarming behaviour in parts
    of the earlier history and during 2022-2024.
  - Published the comparison to the original frozen Round-B models; the
    apparent winner depends on period and methodology.

FINAL V6 AUDIT - Methodological closure (final_v6_audit.py)
  Outputs: v6_final_audit/ and v6_final_* files
  - Recomputed the Round-C walk-forward procedure with a more conservative
    30-month label maturity proxy, rather than 18 months.
  - Examined chronological intercept-only recalibration and an explicitly
    fixed 50% shrinkage toward an expanding historical base rate.
  - Assessed performance by era: 1976-88, 1990-2003, 2005-11,
    2012-21 and 2022-24.
  - Attempted vintage-origin training using each historical training row's
    as-recorded ALFRED feature snapshot from 1999 onward.
  - The origin-vintage training series is much shorter and NOT equivalent
    to reconstructing a complete as-of training database at each refit.
  - The final data have already been examined repeatedly. The final audit
    is a methodological stress test, NOT a new unseen holdout.


4. REPRODUCED KEY RESULTS
------------------------
METRIC DEFINITIONS:
  Brier score = mean squared error of predicted event probabilities (lower
  is better). Log loss also penalizes badly confident wrong probabilities.
  ROC AUC evaluates ranking, not probability calibration. An alert threshold
  of 20% was used for historical warning diagnostics; it is not a vetted
  operational action cutoff. Monthly 3/6/12-month labels overlap heavily;
  their row counts are NOT numbers of independent recession events.

A. INITIAL FIVE-FEATURE LOGISTIC - 12-MONTH HORIZON
  Fixed 1990-2003 revised validation (152 eligible months):
    Historical base rate                 Brier 0.11183
    V5 warning-conditioned probability  Brier 0.07270
    Five-feature logistic               Brier 0.05529

  Frozen 2005-2024 PIT test (220 eligible months):
    Historical base rate                 Brier 0.10147
    V5 warning-conditioned probability  Brier 0.09997
    Five-feature logistic               Brier 0.15940

  Conclusion: validation success did NOT reproduce in the later PIT period.

B. BROAD FIVE-FEATURE RANDOM FOREST - 12-MONTH HORIZON
  Broad-model PIT test (220 months):
    Historical base rate                 Brier 0.10173
    Five-feature Random Forest          Brier 0.10211
  A model that barely matches the base should not be called a validated win.

C. ROUND C WALK-FORWARD WITH 18-MONTH LABEL DELAY
  2005-2024 PIT sample, 217 shared months, 12-month Brier:
    Expanding historical base            0.10176
    V5 warning-conditioned probability  0.10545
    Five-feature RF walk-forward         0.11193
    Seven-feature rates RF walk-forward  0.08863
    Eight-feature all RF walk-forward    0.09159
    Frozen seven-feature RF              0.08229
    Frozen eight-feature RF              0.08069

  Frozen eight-feature performance benefited from comparing choices after
  the test interval had been examined. It must not be advertised as an
  independently selected, verified winner.

D. FINAL 30-MONTH-DELAY AUDIT - 12-MONTH HORIZON
  2005-2024 PIT sample, 217 months, 24 POSITIVE MONTHLY LABELS but only
  TWO distinct recession onsets (2008 and 2020). These 24 positive months
  represent 12 pre-onset months per event, not 24 independent recessions.

    Method                            Brier       Log loss
    -------------------------------------------------------
    Historical expanding base         0.10100       0.3584
    V5 warning-conditioned prob.      0.10519       0.3421
    Five-feature RF walk-forward      0.10920       0.3246
    Seven-feature rates RF            0.08753       0.2640
    Eight-feature rates+credit RF     0.08951       0.2745
    Seven-feature chronological cal.  0.08914       0.2669
    Seven-feature half-shrinkage      0.08614       0.2959

  Seven-feature historical PIT ROC AUC: approximately 0.872.
  Seven-feature Brier improvement relative to the expanding base:
     1 - 0.08753/0.10100 = approximately 13.3%.
  Chronological recalibration did not improve Brier/log loss. Fixed half-
  shrinkage slightly improved Brier while worsening log loss; no robust
  calibration fix emerged.

E. ORIGIN-VINTAGE TRAINING (FINAL AUDIT)
  Same 217-month PIT evaluation at 12 months:
    Historical expanding base            Brier 0.10100
    Five-feature origin-vintage RF       Brier 0.11496
    Seven-feature origin-vintage RF      Brier 0.11927
    Eight-feature origin-vintage RF      Brier 0.11898

  None beat the base rate in this short-history experiment. However, this
  cannot isolate data-revision effects: training starts around 1999 and is
  drastically smaller. January 2005 origin-vintage refits can have as few
  as 24 eligible training observations, making the chosen RF leaf size of
  20 a severe restriction. Do NOT claim that vintage revisions alone caused
  the degradation, or that this recreates a complete real-time backtest.

F. HISTORICAL-ERA STABILITY (FINAL 30-MONTH AUDIT)
  12-month Brier; early and validation use revised predictors; later uses PIT:

    Method                   1976-88   1990-03   2005-24 PIT
    -------------------------------------------------------
    Historical base          0.16001   0.11373   0.10100
    V5 conditional rule      0.10266   0.07295   0.10519
    Five-feature RF          0.11746   0.06833   0.10920
    Seven-feature RF         0.13527   0.07310   0.08753
    Eight-feature RF         0.14289   0.07524   0.08951

  The seven/eight-feature models were NOT consistently the best across eras.
  During 1976-88, at the same 20% threshold, the 7-feature model flagged
  77/110 negative months and the 8-feature model flagged 82/110 negative
  months. Financial signal relationships and calibration are regime-sensitive.

G. 2022-2024 FALSE WARNING DIAGNOSTIC (FINAL 30-MONTH AUDIT)
  The PIT evaluation includes 36 expansion months with zero 12-month-onset
  positives during 2022-2024. At the 12-month horizon:
    Historical base:  average probability 14.9%, Brier 0.02224.
    Seven-feature RF: average probability 35.5%, Brier 0.17358.
    Eight-feature RF: average probability 34.1%, Brier 0.15572.
    Both RF variants flagged 25/36 months at the 20% threshold.

  These sustained warnings are a major reason the system is not considered
  reliable as a present-day recession probability product.

H. SIX-MONTH WARNING COMPARISON (FINAL 30-MONTH AUDIT)
  2005-2024 PIT, fixed 20% threshold; only 2 distinct onsets:
    V5 binary Treasury/housing rule: 1/2 detected, 42 false-warning months.
    Seven-feature RF:              2/2 detected, 30 false-warning months.
    Eight-feature RF:              2/2 detected, 20 false-warning months.

  Historical warning improvement exists, but event counts are too small and
  the period was repeatedly examined. The eight-feature 6-month result is
  neither a guaranteed early-warning system nor a live-track-record claim.


5. WHAT V6 PROVED, AND WHAT IT DID NOT
-------------------------------------
SUPPORTED RESEARCH FINDINGS:
  - The interest-rate additions contain information that improved some
    historical recession-onset probability scores and rankings.
  - Separating training/validation/test chronologically is necessary but
    insufficient if the same evaluation period is repeatedly used to choose
    modelling ideas.
  - Revisions, release timing, model-training data and model selection all
    affect what a retrospective economic forecast appears to have known.
  - Forecast ranking and calibrated probabilities are different objectives.
  - Model performance and false-warning behaviour differ by economic regime.
  - A simple honest benchmark can outperform complex models for important
    periods. Failure to deploy is a legitimate model-risk decision.

NOT SUPPORTED:
  - Reliable or well-calibrated real-time 3/6/12-month recession probabilities.
  - A complete point-in-time trained backtest all the way to 1962.
  - An independent final test set unaffected by earlier exploratory research.
  - Generalizable proof from two later-period recessions or overlapping months.
  - An operational recession alert threshold, future accuracy guarantee,
    investment alpha, stock-market crash prediction or profitable strategy.

FINAL PRODUCT DECISION:
  Do not present the experimental V6 probabilities as validated live output.
  Archive the model experiments, manifests, cached source files, prediction
  records and detailed research audits. Reuse only descriptive economic
  features, robust data services and transparently labelled rule examples
  in the V7 application.

7. REPRODUCTION AND REVIEW NOTES
--------------------------------
The installed scripts include source-level checks and some built-in unit
routines. From the ORIGINAL trusted V6 working directory, and with all
required input data/caches and dependencies present, representative commands:

  python -m unittest discover -s tests -p "test_*.py" -v
  python final_v6_audit.py --self-test
  python final_v6_audit.py --fetch-old-vintages

The last command may make FRED/ALFRED API requests for missing vintages and
writes derived reports. Running it requires the local private FRED API key.
The report here summarizes uploaded real experiment outputs; it does not
claim to rerun the complete V6 training/test sequence afresh.

Critical interpretation safeguards:
  - An ALFRED calendar-date vintage is not guaranteed intraday release timing.
  - Revised predictors cannot be treated as historical information just
    because observation reference dates are filtered.
  - The initial 18-month and final 30-month target-maturity delays are
    approximations, not exact NBER announcement calendars.
  - Final origin-vintage rows do not recreate full contemporaneous refit
    training tables, and the origin-vintage set has much less training history.
  - Two evaluated later-period recessions are far too few to justify reliable
    prospective skill, regardless of overlapping positive monthly labels.
  - Candidate model selection after inspecting 2005-2024 prevents this
    period from being described as an untouched test holdout.


8. EXACT HANDOFF TO V7
----------------------
THE ROADMAP CHANGED:
  Earlier planning anticipated additional forecasting/model-validation phases
  after V6. Following the final audit, the decision was to stop repeated
  recession-probability fitting and make V7 the final polished application.

V7 PRODUCT:
  MacroScript - U.S. Macroeconomic Intelligence & Historical Research.
  Display current FRED observations and selected-date ALFRED data with clear
  reference dates, sources, units, missingness and freshness. Build individual
  and combined explanations from transparent economic feature calculations.
  Show V5/V6 methodological results as a labelled historical Research Lab.

V7 REUSES:
  - Canonical FRED client, ALFRED historical reader and data-quality code.
  - V4 current/Time Machine/revised Research loader and five main features.
  - V5 transparent rule definitions, separated from predictive claims.
  - V5 monthly PIT and revised research CSVs, using only appropriately
    labelled fields and never exposing hindsight outcomes in an as-of view.
  - V6 final reports and historical findings for educational research display.

V7 DOES NOT REUSE AS LIVE FORECASTING INFRASTRUCTURE:
  - The V6 logistic/Random Forest/boosting/probit probability pipelines.
  - V6 hindsight recession targets for current or historical 'what was
    known then' insight generation.
  - Unvalidated absolute risk probabilities or invented recession scores.

V7 IMPLEMENTATION ORDER:
  1. Consolidate canonical V1-V5 backend modules and test imports/logic.
  2. Make a safe, explicit current/history/revised data interface, with
     date-level provenance and cache boundaries.
  3. Build a professional GUI: Current Economy, Time Machine, individual
     and combined Economic Insights, and historical Research Lab.
  4. Test source/date availability, missing data, reference-month alignment,
     revision comparisons and transparent signal calculations.
  5. Package, document, screenshot and release a reproducible V7 application.

The existing V1-V6 version folders stay untouched. V7 is a clean new folder,
not a renamed V6 experiment directory.


9. BOTTOM LINE
--------------
V6 made a meaningful quantitative research contribution but did not meet the
higher standard required to deploy a dependable recession-probability product.
The initial model's impressive validation accuracy was not robust to later
historical data. Additional rates and credit indicators improved selected
historical PIT results, but the gains depended on era, training vintage,
calibration choices and a repeatedly examined test period. Recent false
warnings remained substantial. The responsible decision is to preserve the
complete research evidence, avoid deploying unsupported probabilities, and
finish MacroScript as a polished economic-data and historical-intelligence
tool in V7.
