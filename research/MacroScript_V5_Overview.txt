MACROSCRIPT - VERSION 5 OVERVIEW
================================
Completion date: 2026-11-06
Version status: Research and historical-audit phase complete; NOT a trained forecasting model.

ONE-SENTENCE SUMMARY
--------------------
V5 converted V4's descriptive macroeconomic features into a defined recession-
onset research dataset, tested transparent early-warning rules, checked how
historical revisions and release delays affect those rules, and established
benchmarks and limitations for the probabilistic modelling work in V6.


1. WHY WE BUILT V5
------------------
V1-V4 connected to FRED/ALFRED, retrieved present and historical observations,
verified quality and release/vintage caveats, and computed consistent monthly
features. None of that established predictive usefulness.

V5 asked:
  - What exactly are we forecasting and how should success be measured?
  - Which economic indicators warned ahead of US recession onsets?
  - Does combining indicators reduce false alarms while preserving lead time?
  - Would historical warnings actually have been visible at that decision date?
  - What are the strongest simple rules that V6 should try to beat?

V5 deliberately did NOT fit a statistical forecasting model, generate
calibrated recession probabilities, or validate an investment strategy.


2. THE TARGET AND THE DATA
--------------------------
Prediction outcome (targets.py): NBER-dated US recession onset, evaluated using
FRED USREC monthly labels. Separate targets are recession onset within the
NEXT 3, 6, and 12 calendar months. An expansion month is a prediction-eligible
month; an already-recession month has no onset forecast target. Missing or
unknown outcome horizons must not silently become negative labels. USREC is
NEVER a predictive economic feature.

Five established predictor groups (six source series):
  1. Unemployment / Sahm-style change: UNRATE.
  2. Nonfarm payroll growth: PAYEMS.
  3. Industrial production growth: INDPRO.
  4. Housing permit growth: PERMIT.
  5. Monthly 10y-minus-3m Treasury term spread: GS10 - TB3MS.

IMPORTANT: GS10 - TB3MS is a monthly yield spread in percentage points. It is
not the same series as daily T10Y3M. Annual changes in housing are percentage
changes, while the Treasury threshold is a percentage-point spread.

Two historical datasets must stay separate:

A. RETROSPECTIVE REVISED RESEARCH
   File: v5_research_revised.csv
   756 monthly records, January 1962-December 2024, with five primary feature
   groups and 3/6/12-month outcomes. Eight recession episodes appear in the
   period: 1970, 1973-75, 1980, 1981-82, 1990-91, 2001, 2007-09, and 2020.
   IMPORTANT: these are today's revised historical observations. They were NOT
   necessarily published or available in the same reference month.

B. HISTORICAL POINT-IN-TIME / ALFRED
   File: v5_pit_full_rows.csv
   304 end-of-month snapshots, September 1999-December 2024, covering 2001,
   2008, and 2020 recession onsets. After targeted repair, all 304 snapshots
   have all six underlying source-quality flags marked OK. The snapshots
   reconstruct historically recorded vintages as of each monthly cutoff;
   reference months often lag the decision month. Daily vintages do not prove
   intraday publication timestamps or a completely authentic live replay.

The research label can be determined with hindsight. It is used to evaluate
predictions, never to generate a contemporaneous feature.


3. THE EXPERIMENTS AND WHAT THEY TAUGHT US
-----------------------------------------
STEP 1 - Recession target definitions (targets.py, test_targets.py)
  - Defined eligible expansion months and onset-within-3/6/12-month labels.
  - Checked actual USREC recession periods, and isolated the target from the
    economic predictor set to prevent obvious outcome leakage.

STEP 2 - Historical research datasets (research.py, test_research.py)
  - Constructed a common feature/outcome table and pre-recession windows.
  - Created a consistent way to examine individual recession episodes.
  - Explicitly separated revised-history research and time-machine vintages.

STEP 3 - Individual indicators and simple AND/OR rules
  Files: signals.py, v5_signal_comparison.csv, v5_signal_event_details.csv
  - Studied recession-event detection, first warning lead, monthly false alarms,
    missing-data coverage, and warning spells.
  - Treasury spread and housing permits appeared especially promising as a
    combined *early-warning* rule. Payrolls, industrial production and the
    Sahm-style signal were retained as continuous features; their particular
    standalone thresholds were not the leading 12-month early-warning rules.
  - Lower false-alarm rates do NOT establish a better rule if it misses events.

STEP 4 - Historical accuracy checks and repair
  Files: pit_audit.py, pit_full.py, pit_repair.py, v5_pit_full_*.csv,
         v5_pit_cache/
  - Collected the full 304-month historic vintage dataset and repaired bad
    snapshots rather than silently using incomplete economic observations.
  - Compared same-decision-date and, where supported, same-reference-month
    readings to distinguish release lags from revisions.
  - Found that different release availability can alter historical signals;
    revised economic history must NOT be described as real-time predictions.

STEP 5 - Persistence rules
  Files: persistence.py, v5_persistence_*.csv
  - Compared 1-, 2-, and 3-consecutive-month warning confirmations.
  - Requiring three consecutive months improved the 1999-2024 point-in-time
    relaxed Treasury-plus-housing rule's FPR from 12.9% to 10.5%, but shortened
    mean lead time from 11.7 to 9.7 months.
  - A stricter Treasury-below-0 PLUS housing rule lost one of three recession
    detections at 2- or 3-month persistence.
  - Multi-month waiting alone did not solve prolonged 2022-24 false warnings.

STEP 6 - Corporate credit challenger
  Files: credit_experiment.py, v5_credit_revised_features.csv,
         v5_credit_revised_metrics.csv, v5_credit_pit_pilot.csv
  - Constructed monthly Baa corporate bond yield minus GS10.
  - Historical-vintage pilot succeeded at four selected dates.
  - Revised full-sample spread >=3.0 pp flagged 0/8 recession onsets in advance;
    three-month spread widening >=0.5 pp flagged 3/8.
  - At these TESTED thresholds, credit was not a useful mandatory additional
    early-warning filter. It may still provide economic/financial-stress
    information as a continuous feature for later research.
  - No continuous full 304-month credit-vintage archive was collected in V5.

STEP 7 - Jobless claims plus combination-improvement research
  Files: claims_experiment.py, v5_claims_combo_*.csv,
         v5_claims_revised_features.csv, v5_claims_pit_pilot.csv
  - Built monthly indicators from the latest available weekly IC4WSA four-week
    average: 3-month and 12-month growth.
  - Combined claims and credit confirmations with the fixed Treasury + housing
    baseline, comparing baseline and challenger on matching usable months.
  - In revised 1962-2024 data, Treasury+housing+claims YoY>=0 showed 8/8
    detection and a 4.0% FPR, compared with 7.6% for the baseline on identical
    claims-available months (89.3% of all eligible expansion months covered).
  - This looked strong RETROSPECTIVELY, but did not establish real-time skill.

STEP 8 - Full jobless claims PIT audit with integrated automatic repair
  Files: v5_claims_pit_cache/, v5_claims_pit_full_*.csv
  - Completed 187/187 historical claims snapshots, June 2009-December 2024;
    all are recorded as OK after resumable retries. No separate claims repair
    script was needed.
  - The archive window contains only the 2020 recession onset, so this is a
    source-availability check plus a limited historical rule evaluation.
  - The YEAR-OVER-YEAR claims rule failed to detect that one onset despite its
    excellent retrospective 8/8 result: do not promote it as validated.
  - The THREE-MONTH claims growth >=0 rule retained 1/1 detection, reduced
    monthly false-alarm rate, and shortened first-warning lead.
  - The filtered 3-month rule nevertheless generated MORE separate false-warning
    episodes: lower false-alarm-month percentages are not the whole story.
  - Claims vintage changes were observed versus the revised-date comparison;
    both revisions and different selected reference weeks can matter.


4. REPRODUCED KEY RESULTS
------------------------
All rates below are MONTHLY false-warning frequencies among eligible expansion
months with NO recession onset in the next 12 months. They are NOT the
percentage of recession forecasts that are wrong, nor the percentage of
recessions missed. 'Detected' means at least one flagged expansion month within
the preceding 12 months. Average lead is time from the FIRST qualifying flag
in that evaluation window to onset.

REVISED-HISTORY RESEARCH, 1962-2024 (8 recession episodes)
  Treasury spread < 0:
     7/8 detected; FPR 6.4%; mean lead 9.7 months.
  Treasury spread < 0.5 AND housing permits YoY < 0:
     8/8 detected; FPR 10.6% (61/575 negative months);
     mean lead 10.5 months.
  Add claims YoY >= 0 (only matched claims-available months):
     8/8 detected; challenger FPR 4.0% vs matched baseline FPR 7.6%;
     mean lead 9.0 months; 89.3% coverage of full eligible months.

POINT-IN-TIME, 1999-2024 (304 snapshots, 3 recession onsets)
  Treasury spread < 0:
     3/3 detected; FPR 12.1%; mean lead 9.0 months.
  Treasury spread < 0.5 AND housing permits YoY < 0:
     3/3 detected; FPR 12.9% (31/240 negative months);
     mean lead 11.7 months.
  Both reported rules have 100% usable eligible-month coverage in this audit.

CLAIMS POINT-IN-TIME, 2009-2024 (187 snapshots; 1 onset, 2020)
  Baseline Treasury + housing:
     1/1 detected; FPR 13.4% (23/172 negative months);
     first warning 12 months ahead; 2 separate false-warning runs.
  Baseline AND claims YoY >= 0:
     0/1 detected; FPR 7.6% (13/172); 4 false-warning runs.
  Baseline AND claims 3-month growth >= 0:
     1/1 detected; FPR 5.8% (10/172); first warning 9 months ahead;
     5 separate false-warning runs.
  All 184 eligible expansion months in this claims audit had usable features.

These figures are research observations, not a guarantee of how any rule
performs in the next recession. In particular, COVID-19 was an exogenous shock;
a prior warning does not establish that the model foresaw the pandemic.


5. FINAL V5 BENCHMARKS TO FREEZE
--------------------------------
PRIMARY RULE BENCHMARK:
   Treasury spread (GS10 - TB3MS) < +0.5 percentage points
   AND housing permits 12-month growth < 0%.

SIMPLE BENCHMARK:
   Treasury spread (GS10 - TB3MS) < 0 percentage points.

OPTIONAL EXPERIMENTAL CONFIRMATION:
   Primary rule AND IC4WSA claims 3-month growth >= 0%.
   Evaluate only on historically supported claims dates; do not represent
   this rule as proven on all eight historical recessions.

The three rules above are reference comparators, NOT the final predictive
model. They were investigated/selected using V5's observed history and are
not untouched out-of-sample benchmarks.

7. WHAT V5 CANNOT CLAIM
-----------------------
  - No model has been trained and no calibrated probabilities exist yet.
  - Eight recessions is a tiny number of independent economic episodes;
    hundreds of overlapping monthly rows are not independent recessions.
  - Most rule thresholds were explored after seeing the historical sample.
    V5's 8/8 is in-sample selection, not out-of-sample validation.
  - Long revised-history data contain information that may have arrived later.
    They are useful for feature research, not a strict live-deployment replay.
  - ALFRED month-end vintages are stronger evidence than revised records but
    are not proof that a release was available at every intraday cutoff.
  - The IC4WSA archive does not provide directly usable pre-May-2009 historical
    vintages for the 2001 or 2008 claims-component decisions.
  - Claims 2009-24 validation covers ONE onset only. A filtered 1/1 result
    is not strong proof of future sensitivity.
  - False-alarm months, separate warning episodes, the time spent in warning,
    and prediction probabilities are different metrics.
  - Predicting US recession onset is different from predicting stock-market
    returns, equity drawdowns, or a trading decision.


8. EXACT HANDOFF TO V6
----------------------
V6 GOAL:
   Train an interpretable statistical model that outputs recession-onset
   probabilities within the next 3, 6 and 12 months. Compare it against a
   constant historical event-rate probability benchmark and the frozen V5
   rule benchmarks on chronologically held-out observations.

WHAT CARRIES OVER:
   - Core V4 feature definitions and V5 historical feature rows.
   - V5's fixed, separate 3/6/12-month recession targets and USREC labels.
   - Historical-vintage source/quality/reference-date safeguards.
   - Three frozen V5 warning-rule comparators.
   - Claims and credit continuous features as optional research extensions,
     only where vintage and training-date coverage permit.
   - All V5 unit tests and reproducibility/audit reports.

WHAT DOES NOT CARRY OVER AS A CLAIM:
   - '8/8 implies reliable future forecasting.'
   - Claims YoY was validated historically (it was not).
   - In-sample rule selection is a fair untouched test set.
   - Credit or extra claims are mandatory predictors because we tested them.

SUGGESTED V6 IMPLEMENTATION SEQUENCE (NOT MORE V5 THRESHOLD SEARCHING):
   1. Build a strict modelling matrix for the three horizons: features,
      targets, observation/reference dates, missingness and source flags.
   2. Train separate regularized logistic regressions for 3/6/12 months.
      Fit scaling/imputation in training folds ONLY; preserve feature names
      and report interpretable coefficients/feature contributions.
   3. Chronologically evaluate forecasts. Prevent leakage from overlapping
      target horizons and training labels that extend past the forecast date.
      Start with training-only recession base-rate probabilities; estimate
      V5-rule forecast probabilities from training data only. Compare models
      on identical eligible test months and horizons.
   4. Report Brier score, log loss, discrimination, calibration limitations,
      recession-event detection, warning lead, false-warning months AND runs,
      model coverage, and uncertainty. Freeze thresholds before evaluating
      test periods. Distinguish revised-source chronology from PIT audits.
   5. Save reproducible probability predictions, model metadata, evaluation
      reports, and a V6 overview. Only then consider V7 deeper robustness
      analysis and V8 app/presentation work.

SUCCESS CRITERION:
   Not '8/8 in the data we tuned against.' A successful V6 is an honest,
   reproducible forecast system whose probability quality and warning
   trade-offs compare favourably with appropriately trained simple baselines
   on chronologically evaluated data. If a complex model does not outperform
   the simple V5 rules, report it: that is a valid research finding.

CAUTION ABOUT EARLIER ROADMAP:
   The original V1-V8 project roadmap assigned model training to V5. During
   implementation, the schedule evolved. The V4 overview and present handoff
   use the updated division: V5 = research/benchmarks, V6 = probability models,
   V7 = deeper out-of-sample validation, V8 = finished application.


9. BOTTOM LINE
--------------
We started V5 with calculated economic measurements but no empirical evidence
of their predictive usefulness. We finished with labeled recession horizons,
a 63-year revised research table, a repaired 304-month core point-in-time
archive, a 187-month claims point-in-time archive with automatic repair, tested
rules with explicit false-alarm/lead-time trade-offs, and a clear baseline for
V6. We did NOT finish with a proven recession forecasting model.

The next version must learn probabilities from the features and evaluate them,
not simply keep adjusting threshold combinations until historical numbers look
better.
